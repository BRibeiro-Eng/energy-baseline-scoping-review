## 1. What is being amended, and why

The registered charting instrument for the analytical corpus is the Level-B variable set B1–B14 (protocol v2 §6, reproduced in the operational protocol at §6.2, schema FROZEN at v2.2.1). That instrument was executed over the corpus. On the 42-paper Corpus-B pre-fill it failed, measurably, on four of its fields:

| Registered field | Measured failure |
|---|---|
| B6 `specific_limitations` | The enum cannot hold the answer to SQ2. Across the Level-B pre-fill records, 45 of 49 limitation records code `other`; at paper level, 15 of 18 limitation-bearing papers code `other`. Zero `fouling`, zero `catalyst deactivation` — the two mechanisms the thesis was built to investigate returned nothing, not because the literature is silent but because the enum sends every observation to `other`. |
| B5 `identifies_model_limitations` | Catches study limitations as model limitations. *"Further research is suggested"* and *"I cannot get data from all factories"* were coded as model limitations. Surface disagreement: claude True 19/32 against chatgpt True 30/41. |
| B1 `normalisation_variables` | Granularity unresolvable. One surface decomposes, the other aggregates; 237 divergences were wording, not substance. |
| B13b `performance_metrics_reported` | An empty list is ambiguous — empty for 7 of 31 papers, with "fitted but unreported" indistinguishable from "unfitted, nothing to report". |

Three further objects the synthesis requires were captured by no registered field: what stands on the left-hand side of the model; whether the energy quantity is actually metered at the studied boundary; and whether the baseline is ever rebuilt. The third is the question the refinery case turns on.

Amending the instrument is therefore necessary. The registered v2.2.1 schema **remains frozen and is not edited**. Stage C is a separate, separately versioned output (`v2.3.0-C`); the Tier-1 adjudication record and the corpus-wide grounding result already obtained under v2.2.1 stand as they are. The field-by-field B1–B14 → Stage C correspondence is tabulated in `RESULT_FIELD_VERIFICATION_MATRIX_2026-09-12.md` §2; no v2.2.1 field is deleted from the record, and v2.3.0-C records carry their own `prompt_tag` so that no v2.2.1 figure is re-labelled as a v2.3.0-C figure.

## 2. The changes, each against the registered text it departs from

### 2.1 Stage C as a separate versioned output

**Registered text.** Protocol v2 §6 / operational §6.2: the Level-B variable set B1–B14, "Schema FROZEN at v2.2.1".

**Change.** A new charting form, `stage_c/schema.json`, `schema_version` const `2.3.0-C`, valid against JSON Schema draft 2020-12, `additionalProperties: false` throughout. The SHA-256 recorded in `stage_c/SCHEMA_SHA256.txt` on 2026-09-12, and of the file as it stood when this amendment was prepared, is `d855254b73a8640b4e13255876a3021ae9da699e1bf04bb4db3d5b2444e883ff`. That same line currently carries the marker `NOT FROZEN`: the freeze is executed once the decisions the pilot put to the reviewer are closed (§2.11), and the digest of the frozen file is the digest of record. **The freeze is dated 2026-09-13**, the date of this deposit, and the digest of record is the one above: `d855254b73a8640b4e13255876a3021ae9da699e1bf04bb4db3d5b2444e883ff`. `stage_c/schema.json` did not change between 2026-09-12 and the freeze. On the freeze, `stage_c/SCHEMA_SHA256.txt` carries that digest and that date without the `NOT FROZEN` marker, the pre-freeze file is kept beside it under a dated name, and every subsequent validator call exits on a digest mismatch — which is the enforcement, not the promise. v2.2.1 is untouched and remains the instrument of record for Corpus-A Level-A results.

### 2.2 Unit of extraction

**Registered text.** One charted record per paper; Level-B fields are article-level variables.

**Change.** The unit becomes **paper → `applications[]` → `models[]`**. An application is one studied context (a site, a unit, a defined multi-site population); a model is one energy performance model — one dependent variable, one functional form, one variable set, one set of metrics. C1–C8, the model parameters and the data requirements hang off the model. Mechanisms and adaptations remain at paper level but carry `refers_to` (a `model_id`, an `application_id`, or the literal `paper-level argument`).

**Why.** The article-level row is not a style preference, it is an error: a paper fitting both a regression and a neural network had the R² of one model and the variables of the other in the same row. Aggregation back to article level is declared — each mechanism counts **once per paper** regardless of how many models it affects. The pilot exercised this: 21 models over 6 applications across five papers, including 6 models on one petrochemical paper and 5 models over 2 applications on one glass and cast-iron paper (§2.11).

### 2.3 Field set C1–C13, and the derivation status of its new enum values

**Registered text.** B1–B14 as listed at §6.2, including B6 `specific_limitations` = "non-stationarity / fouling / catalyst deactivation / feedstock variability / other (specify in evidence)" and B9 `adaptation_types` = "dynamic time window / segmented baseline / hybrid physical model / other (specify in evidence)".

**Change.** Thirteen fields, C1–C13. C9 `mechanisms` **splits B6 into two axes**, because cause and effect were competing for one slot: `mechanism_cause` (11 values) and `mechanism_effect_on_model` (13 values). The effect axis retains the four registered values `non-stationarity`, `fouling`, `catalyst deactivation`, `feedstock variability` and adds eight: `observability gap`, `multi-product allocation`, `baseload` (baseload or non-productive energy), `validity envelope` (partial load or validity envelope), `statistical misspecification`, `baseline drift` (baseline drift or temporal validity), `cross-site comparability`, `interaction with improvement programmes`, plus `other`. C11 `adaptations` carries eleven values — `rolling window` (the registered "dynamic time window"), `segmented baseline`, `change-point`, `additional normalisation variable`, `hybrid physical-statistical` (the registered "hybrid physical model"), `online re-estimation`, `outlier exclusion`, `weighted estimation`, `normalisation to a reference product`, `improved metering`, `other` — each with `status` (proposed / implemented / evaluated) and `addresses_mechanism`, the latter recorded **only when the paper states the link** and never inferred. C10 gives study limitations their own five-value field so they stop inflating SQ2; `future work only` is not a limitation and is not recorded at all. C12 records what a method needs to be reproduced; the transferability verdict is the reviewer's and is not an extraction field. C13 records four reproducibility items as yes / partial / not reported / not applicable, and no reproducibility index is computed from them.

**The derivation status, stated plainly.** The enum values added to C9 and C11 **were derived from the AI pre-fill over the locked 42-paper roster, not from my own reading of those papers.** An earlier draft of the Stage-C specification claimed the field set was "pre-specified, not fitted to results". That claim was false as written and is withdrawn. The correct status is a **corpus-informed revision of the charting form, frozen before re-extraction**: the categories were abstracted from observations the machine made over the corpus the form will then be applied to, and AI-originated observations are not independent of those data. JBI guidance on scoping reviews, and the iterative-charting step of Levac et al. (2010), permit a charting form to be refined in contact with the corpus **provided the refinement is reported**; it is reported here, with its provenance, and the order is enforced mechanically rather than by memory: `output/stage_c_run.py` refuses to start unless `stage_c/AMENDMENT_FILED.txt` exists and is non-empty, and that file carries the deposit date of this amendment. Tested: it refuses.

Two consequences follow and are accepted. First, frequency counts over the eight added effect categories are **descriptive of this corpus** and carry no claim of category completeness for the wider literature. Second, two categories — `cross-site comparability` and `interaction with improvement programmes` — have n = 1 in the pre-fill and are kept on conceptual, not frequency, grounds: a revamp or a catalyst change perturbing a baseline is exactly the second category, and it is central to the refinery case even if rare in this corpus. That is a stated judgement, not a measured result. The pilot then raised the same question again about the **cause** axis, where seven of thirty occurrences coded `other` against four used enum values; whether those verbatim clusters become enum values or stay in `other` is a decision owed before the freeze and is recorded at §2.11, not settled here.

### 2.4 `claim_status`, `support_class`, and three kinds of absence

**Registered text.** §6.3: an empty multi-select or a `"none"` value may carry `evidence_quote: null` or a literal substring stating the absence.

**Change, three parts.**

1. **`claim_status`** on every mechanism occurrence: `general` / `applied to the case` / `empirically demonstrated`. Only the latter two count toward the SQ2 headline figure. The draft specification anchored `statistical misspecification` on *"When the error variance is not constant, the error term is called heteroscedastic"* — a textbook definition, which does not show that the paper found heteroscedasticity in its own model. Every mechanism family in the chapter is re-counted under this rule, and the effect is not cosmetic: on the five pilot papers the rule excluded **8 of 30 mechanism occurrences (27 %)** from the counting subset, where the v2.2.1 instrument would have counted all 30. That is a measurement of the correction on five papers, not a claim about the corpus; whether each of the 8 is correctly classified is a human judgement and is one of the pilot's open items (§2.11).
2. **`support_class`** on every value: `direct statement` (the quote alone states the value) or `requires inference` (reasoning, domain knowledge or another part of the paper was combined in). Every `requires inference` value routes to me and **never enters a headline figure**. On the pilot, 130 values were marked `requires inference`.
3. **Three kinds of absence** replacing the ambiguous `none`: `not reported` (the paper is silent), `not applicable` (meaningless here), `explicitly absent` (the paper states the thing does not exist or was not done). The first two require an `absence_basis` — `searched full text` / `section absent` / `explicitly stated absent` — with optional `searched_terms`, and carry **no quote**. The validator rejects a quote attached to a `not reported` value: an absence justified by a fabricated quote is worse than a gap, because it looks verified. On the pilot, 184 declared absences produced 0 problems under this rule.

### 2.5 Grounding — exact acceptance, two new normalisation rules, locator correction, and one check that is not machine-checkable

**Registered text.** §6.3: "The verifier then uses `rapidfuzz.fuzz.partial_ratio` with length-sensitive thresholds (≥95 / ≥90 / ≥85 for short/medium/long quotes)", over an eight-rule normalisation.

**Change.** Grounding is specified as **three checks**, not one, implemented in `output/stage_c_grounding.py`. `output/grounding.py` is not modified, because its behaviour is in the provenance of the v2.2.1 corpus-wide result and changing it would invalidate that result.

- **G1 — the excerpt exists.** Acceptance becomes **exact match after normalisation**. Fuzzy scoring is retained **only** to surface near-misses as review candidates; it never accepts. Two normalisation rules are added to the registered eight, both from measured failures, not from anticipation: **rule 9**, inline line numbers stripped, because 18 corpus papers are manuscripts with line numbers interleaved into the extracted text; **rule 10**, column interleaving tolerated as an in-order subsequence with a bounded insertion budget, because `rayyan-472536601` is a two-column PDF whose extraction reads across columns and whose genuinely verbatim quotes scored 66–79. Rule-10 acceptances are reported as their own class and are never merged with clean exact matches. Without these two rules, exact matching rejects true quotes and the checker becomes a false-alarm generator.
- **G2 — the locator is right.** A quote found on another page is reported as a locator error, not as a fabrication. Under the correction rule below, a unique other-page match is corrected, flagged and queued rather than discarded; a quote found on several pages, or shorter than the minimum length, stays a blocking failure.
- **G3 — the excerpt supports the value.** **Not machine-checkable, and not claimed to be.** It is reported as counts by `support_class`, with every `requires inference` value routed to me. No script in this pipeline claims G3 passed.

**Deviation 3, 2026-09-12 — the grounding check may correct a page locator.** The registered procedure has no provision for a machine altering a recorded locator; it accepts or it fails. The change: where a quote exists word for word on **exactly one** page and the extracting surface cited another, `output/stage_c_grounding.py` replaces the page in the record, keeps the page as the surface wrote it in `page_as_recorded`, sets `locator_corrected: true`, queues the cell for human review, and counts it in its own class beside the exact count — **never as an exact match, and never folded into one**. G1 passes, G2 fails, and the record says so. The correction is mechanical and independently re-checkable, because the string match is its own evidence. Four guards, because this is a machine touching provenance: the match must be **unique** (a quote found on two pages stays a failure); the quote must be at least **40 characters** (`LOCATOR_MIN_CHARS` in the grounding module), below which a phrase can recur in a running header or caption; **both page numbers stay in the record**; and a surface that writes `locator_corrected` or `page_as_recorded` itself is a **validation error** (`output/stage_c_validate.py`), because the flag is the checker's finding and not the extractor's claim.

**The measured reason for the rule.** On the pilot this was the dominant failure mode: of the four papers that needed retries, two failed on nothing else, always by exactly one page, in both directions, clustered on figure-heavy pages. Treating it as blocking spent three CLI attempts and a whole extraction on a record whose evidence was entirely genuine, which was then quarantined. The cost of refusing to correct was therefore measured, and it was paid in discarded true records; the cost of correcting is that one page number in the record is the checker's rather than the extractor's, which is why it is flagged, counted, kept alongside the original and queued for a human. On the five final pilot records the class is empty — 538 quotes exact, 0 wrong page, 0 not found, 0 locator corrections — so on records that validate cleanly the rule costs the reviewer nothing. The two figures describe different things and both are now reported. The records carry **24** corrected locators (23 on `rayyan-472535351`, 1 on `rayyan-472536612`); of those, **3** sit on cells a registered result reads, which is why matrix §3b counts 3 among the P1 routing reasons. The run report's per-pass counter reads 0 because a correction written into the record makes the next pass find the quote on the page it now cites, so the report prints both counts: `locator_corr=0(+23 from earlier passes)`. On `rayyan-472535351` the 23 split 9 cells cited one page late and 14 cited one page early — both directions on one paper, on its figure-heavy pages..

**Restatement of the earlier corpus-wide grounding figure.** The figure previously stated as "corpus-wide grounding: 7 075 / 7 075" is withdrawn as a measure of corpus coverage. Two facts qualify it. Acceptance was **fuzzy** at thresholds 95 / 90 / 85, so a 200-character quote could differ by up to 15 % and pass. And a further **2 786 value envelopes carried no quote at all**, which places them entirely outside the guarantee: the 7 075 verified quotes therefore cover **72 %** of value envelopes, not all of them. The underlying v2.2.1 result is not retracted — it remains a true statement about the 7 075 quote-bearing envelopes — but it is restated with its denominator from this point forward.

### 2.6 Deviation 2 — Stage C extracts on one model surface; the registered two-surface partition is not executed

**Registered text.** C-2.3 §3-Step-1 and §3-Step-2: two independent model families chart all available papers against the same schema with versioned prompts, and every (paper, field) cell is partitioned into **concordant** or **divergent**, the divergent set going to the reviewer. And §2, with operational §2: "Inter-model agreement is used **only as a routing signal** to allocate human effort — **never** as a reliability metric. (AI-vs-AI consistency shares model biases and cannot certify correctness.)" Further, §12: no third-model arbiter; metrics reported per field, never pooled; no Cohen's κ, no agreement coefficients.

**Change — reviewer decision, 2026-09-12.** For Stage C the second model family is **not an extractor**. It reviews the instrument, the records and the documents. **Tiers 1 and 3 already ran dual and are untouched**: the 82 Tier-1 blind-first membership adjudications, the §3-Step-3b Level-A divergence adjudications and the §4a SRS frame all stand as executed and keep their own provenance. The change is confined to Stage C.

**What this removes.** There is no second extraction to reconcile. Therefore no Tier-2 (paper, field) cell can be called **concordant** or **divergent**; no **reveal-change rate against a second surface** exists for Stage C; and **no inter-model agreement statistic of any kind is reported for Stage C**. The verification state `provisional — model agreement only` stops occurring: with no second surface to agree with, an unverified cell is plainly *unverified*, which is a clarification of the record rather than a loss.

**What is not weakened, and what is.** No *claim* is weakened, because agreement was never treated as verification: §0 of C-2.3 and the verification matrix admit only the human acts V1 (open the cited page and judge the quote), V2 (read the relevant sections) and V3 (extract blind) as verification, and recorded agreement as `provisional — model agreement only`. What is lost is the **triage**. The divergence set was what told the reviewer which cells to open, and it is gone.

**The registered substitute.** The reviewer's worklist is built from the record's **own declarations of weakness**, implemented in `output/stage_c_triage.py`, mapped cell by cell to the registered results of `RESULT_FIELD_VERIFICATION_MATRIX_2026-09-12.md` §3 and prioritised:

- **P0** — the grounding check routed the cell (interleaved, line-number ambiguity, locator corrected): read it; the instrument asked for a human.
- **P1** — a registered result depends on it **and** it declares itself weak: `support_class: requires inference`, `claim_status: general`, an `other` enum with its verbatim, a corrected locator: read it.
- **P2** — a registered result depends on it **and** it is an absence with a basis and no quote: read the sections, because only reading confirms an absence.
- **P3** — a registered result depends on it, `direct statement`, exact quote: sample.
- **P4** — no registered result depends on it: no human time budgeted.

Measured on the five pilot papers: **171 of 723 cells to read (24 %)**, **130 to sample**, **422 (58 %) not result-bearing** — about **34 cells to read per paper**, on a mean of 145 cells per paper. The P1 reasons, in order: 32 `requires inference`, 8 `claim_status general`, 18 `other` verbatims across three axes, 3 corrected locators. The field → result map inside `output/stage_c_triage.py` is the executable form of matrix §3, editing one without the other is a defect, and the script is what the worklist comes from.

**The trade-off, stated rather than glossed.** Inter-model divergence would have flagged **fewer** cells, because two surfaces agree far more often than one surface hedges — but on a criterion that **shares model biases**, so two models misreading a sentence identically is invisible to it. These signals flag more cells, on a criterion about this extraction's own declared weakness, and every one of them is declared in the record rather than inferred by the pipeline. Neither is verification; both are routing. The P3 sample is the only thing standing between a confidently wrong cell and an unexamined value, and its size is reported.

**The comparator is retained, not deleted.** `output/stage_c_compare.py` and its suite encode nine measured comparator defects and are kept in the repository in case a second extraction surface is ever added; they are **out of the executed pipeline and produce nothing for this review**. Their specification — structure compared first, positional alignment declared as an assumption rather than a finding, no semantic equivalence (non-identical free text stays `unresolved`, never read as absent), no κ and no pooled agreement percentage — remains the specification of that code and is not a claim about anything in this review.

### 2.7 Deviation 1, second part — the registered §3-Step-5 blind-first Level-B design was not executed as registered

**Registered text.** Validation Protocol C-2.3 §2 (Tier 2) and §3-Step-5: "the whole roster is blind-first through |B| = 40; only papers beyond 40 receive AI pre-fill + human field-by-field verification. On blind-first papers the form withholds model outputs until commit". And §6b, on the overrun cut order: "**Never cut:** the FN census, blind-first Tier-1 adjudication, and **the blind-first Tier-2 work prescribed by the active §3-Step-5 branch**."

**The branch was correctly selected.** With the locked |B| = 42, the remainder after the fixed head is 17 papers, so the C-2.3 edge case (remainder ≤ 15 → integral blind-first) does not apply. `n_sub = min(15, 42 − 25) = 15`, drawn **seed 44** without replacement. Registered treatment: **25** papers in sector-priority order (refining first, then petrochemicals, then the remaining sectors; 2020+ first, then recency, then key as final tiebreaker) **plus 15 drawn papers = 40 blind-first**, and **2** papers beyond 40 receiving AI pre-fill plus field-by-field human verification. Registered budget for the branch: 35–55 h. The selected head, the seed-44 draw and the two remainder papers are enumerated by key in `CORPUS_CLOSURE_2026-09-12.md` §5 and reproducible from `output/fn_census_close_corpus.py`.

**It was not executed in that form.** What exists is AI pre-fill over all 42 papers under the frozen v2.2.1 instrument — that pre-fill did run on two model families — with no blind-first human extraction yet committed on any of them. The Stage-C re-extraction that replaces it runs on one surface (§2.6). This is a departure from a provision the registered design names as one it will never cut, and I record it as such rather than as a refinement. The consequence is direct: **the §3-Step-5 blind-first design cannot be claimed for this review as registered.**

**What replaces it — a two-tier human coverage plan, with its selection rules declared.** The registered and executed coverage are tabulated side by side in `RESULT_FIELD_VERIFICATION_MATRIX_2026-09-12.md` §4.

1. **Tier 1 — core, 11 papers.** The refining and petrochemical head of `ROSTER_LOCK_2026-09-12.json`. Full **blind-first** extraction, the form withholding model output until commit. This is where SQ4 and the transferability argument are read. Two cautions attach. Withholding the form does not undo a prior exposure, so the per-paper dossiers for these 11 are generated **only after** their human extractions are committed. And the reveal-change rate describes changes after reveal: it is not a measure of correctness and not a direct measure of anchoring, and it is reported for what it is.
2. **Tier 2 — result-bearing fields, the other 31 papers.** Verification of the fields that enter the SQ2–SQ4 counts, **including absences**, because verifying only what the machine found cannot find what it missed. A true omission check means reading the paper; this tier is costed honestly rather than presented as cheap. The worklist for it is the P0–P2 set of §2.6, with P3 sampled; cells no registered result depends on receive no human time by construction.

**The reason is the calendar, and it is stated as that.** The reduction from 40 papers at blind-first to 11 is not a design improvement and is not presented as one.

**The consequence for what may be claimed.** Under 11 + 31, every **denominator** claim over Corpus B loses its registered basis. R2, R3, R7, R10, R12 and R13 of the verification matrix are denominator claims; "*n* of 42 papers report X", "X is absent from the corpus" and "two thirds propose nothing" all require blind-first coverage of the roster, which the executed design does not have. Such results are reportable only (a) over the **11**, named, with their sector composition given, or (b) over the **42** at field-verification level with the sections read declared — and never as properties of the literature. Roughly **70 %** of the SQ2 mechanism evidence sits outside the 11, so the head does not substitute for the roster. Which result may be claimed at which level, and the cut order if the hours run out, are settled in advance, per result, in `RESULT_FIELD_VERIFICATION_MATRIX_2026-09-12.md` §3–§7.

My stated intention is to read all 42 papers. That intention changes the class of the work for the better — Stage C becomes **pre-fill for my own reading rather than a substitute for it**, which is the hybrid the registered design already contemplates for the remainder beyond 40 — but it is an intention, not a completed instrument, and it is not claimed as one. If Tier 2 does not fit before submission, the chapter reports the two tiers **separately** and says which conclusions rest on human-verified values and which on the exploratory AI mapping. Interim results are reported with human coverage and the AI mapping separated; the final version carries the verified values. The supervisor has been informed of this non-execution. D3 of the C-2.3 decision record deferred supervisor endorsements as non-blocking; being informed is not the same as having endorsed, and no endorsement is claimed here.

**Prior human work that is unaffected and stands as it is.** Tier-1 blind-first adjudication of 82 papers; the corpus-membership adjudication (Corpus A = 331, Corpus B = 42); the FN candidate-union census and its closure; the §3-Step-3b Level-A divergence adjudications and the §4a SRS frame.

### 2.8 Corpus decisions of 2026-09-12, reported as §5.3 requires

**Registered text.** §5.3: "a paper in an unlisted continuous-flow sector is reported as an **exploratory case outside the main Corpus B** unless a **dated, documented corpus decision** (recorded in `run_metadata.json` and noted for the OSF amendment) adds the sector to the main list BEFORE its papers are counted in Corpus-B analyses."

**(a) The main sector list was not expanded; 13 exploratory cases stay outside Corpus B.** Thirteen papers sit in continuous-flow sectors that are not on the §5.3 list: combined-cycle thermal power; ceramics (tile, brick, sanitary ware); municipal sewage treatment; sugar; upstream oil and gas (gathering centres, offshore platforms); non-metallic minerals (gypsum calcination); continuous-production food; wet corn milling. ## 4. What the review does not claim

- That inter-model agreement proves correctness. Agreement is routing and description; AI-vs-AI consistency shares model biases and cannot certify a value.
- **Any inter-model agreement statistic for Stage C.** There is one extraction surface (§2.6), so no Tier-2 cell is concordant or divergent and no reveal-change rate against a second surface exists. Stage-C figures are reported as single-surface extraction plus the human verification actually performed, and nothing else. Tier-1 and Tier-3 dual-extraction figures are unaffected and keep their own provenance.
- That the triage replacing divergence routing measures correctness. It ranks cells by what the extraction **declares about itself**; a cell that declares itself a direct statement with an exact quote can still be wrong, and the sampled P3 tier is the only thing standing between that and an unexamined value. The sampled share is reported.
- That Level-C values are human-verified, outside the papers where I verified them.
- Any per-field error bound on Level-C values. There is **no human reference set** to bound against, so no interval is computed and none is implied.
- **The §3-Step-5 blind-first Level-B design as registered.** It was not executed (§2.7).
- **Any denominator claim over Corpus B under the executed 11 + 31 coverage.** "*n* of 42 papers report X", "X is absent from the corpus", "two thirds propose nothing" require blind-first coverage of the whole roster. R2, R3, R7, R10, R12 and R13 of the verification matrix are claims of that kind; they are reported either over the 11 blind-first papers — named, with their sector composition — or over the 42 at field-verification level with the sections read declared, and never as properties of the literature.
- That the refining and petrochemical head substitutes for the roster on SQ2. Roughly 70 % of the mechanism evidence sits in cement, glass, pulp and paper, and steel; a figure drawn from the 11 is a figure about refining.
- That the C9 mechanism categories are complete for the literature, or that their frequencies are anything other than descriptive of this corpus, given the derivation declared at §2.3.
- That a grounded quote semantically supports the value assigned to it. G1 proves textual traceability and G2 the locator, subject to the correction rule below; G3 rests on human instruments.
- **That every page locator in a Stage-C record is as the extracting surface wrote it.** Under Deviation 3 (§2.5) a unique one-page mismatch is corrected by the grounding check, with the recorded page kept in `page_as_recorded`, `locator_corrected: true`, a review queue entry and a separate count. A corrected locator is a cell whose G2 failed, not a verified cell, and is never counted as an exact match.
- That Corpus-B membership is closed. The census empties the three-detector candidate **union**; a paper invisible to all three detectors escapes it. That residual is argued implausible and is declared, not quantified.
- That the Stratum-II misclassification rate is bounded (§2.8c). It is closed on the highest-probability path and declared as a residual risk elsewhere.
- Equivalence to dual independent **human** extraction. With no second human, "AI unreliable" cannot be separated from "variable intrinsically ambiguous"; test–retest measures self-consistency, not accuracy.
- A certified per-variable κ, or any pooled agreement coefficient. None is computed, per the registered design.
- That the reveal-change rate is a causal estimate of anchoring. It is reported descriptively, for the 11 blind-first papers only, and against the pre-fill rather than against a second extraction surface.
- Coverage of the 137 non-retrieved reports, which remain a separate declared limitation with their own missingness table.

## 5. Limitations

1. **The human-verification gap is the principal limitation of this chapter.** Eleven papers are planned for blind-first reading and 31 for result-bearing-field verification; until Tier 2 completes, the majority of Level-C values rest on a **single-surface AI extraction** with no second surface to agree with and no human reading behind them. They are unverified, and they are labelled unverified.
2. **The human-verified core is not where most of the SQ2 evidence is.** Roughly **70 % of the mechanism evidence sits outside the refining and petrochemical head** — in cement, glass, pulp and paper, steel. The 11 core papers are where the **transferability** argument is read properly; they are not a representative sample of the mechanism evidence, and they must not be reported as if they were.
3. **Seven corpus-closure verdicts carry the provenance shortfall of §2.9**, and the triage dwell and evidence gates were not exercised on them.
4. **The added C9 and C11 categories are corpus-informed and AI-originated** (§2.3). The form is fixed and the order is machine-enforced, but the categories are not independent of the data they will be applied to, and the pilot has already raised the same question for the `mechanism_cause` axis (§2.11).
5. **Grounding bounds what it bounds.** Exact-after-normalisation acceptance proves the excerpt exists. It does not prove the excerpt supports the value; rule-10 acceptances, line-number ambiguities and corrected locators are each weaker classes than a clean exact match and are reported separately; a corrected locator means a page number in the record is the checker's finding rather than the extractor's; and the restated v2.2.1 figure covers 72 % of value envelopes, with 28 % carrying no quote at all.
6. **Surface provenance cannot be controlled to one standard, and Stage C now has only one surface.** The ChatGPT surface, where it was used as an extractor — the v2.2.1 pre-fill, and the Tier-1 and Tier-3 dual work — is driven by hand: one new chat per paper, no pasting of the other surface's output, cross-chat memory disabled, and a per-paper log recording the model name the interface reported. What it cannot provide (enforced clean sessions, captured session identifiers, captured model identity) is a declared provenance limitation of every value it produced. Consumer-subscription surfaces expose no dated model snapshots, no sampling parameters and no request identifiers; the deposited raw outputs and logs, not the live pipeline, are the reproducible object. For Stage C that surface no longer extracts (§2.6), which removes its provenance problem from the Stage-C record and removes the cross-surface signal with it.
7. **Detector (c) is a single-surface instrument with mixed model labels** (§2.10). The Stage-C pilot is likewise single-surface with two model labels reported per run (§2.11).
8. **Residual membership risk outside the candidate union remains declared rather than quantified**, as does the upstream Level-1/2 exclusion risk, which the §3-Step-4b instrument spot-checks rather than closes.
9. **51 Corpus-A papers hold Level-A values from one model surface only** (46 chatgpt, 5 claude) — adjudicated eligible after the other surface short-circuited Level A, so they carry no inter-model divergence signal at all and bypass the reconciliation design. This is a Corpus-A limitation, separate from Stage C, and no queue yet exists for it.
10. **The machine cost is now measured and the human cost is not.** The pilot spent 17 attempts and $20.50 on five papers, at roughly $1.2–1.8 and 7–9 minutes per attempt, of which twelve attempts were instrument defects rather than hard papers; extrapolated at one attempt per paper, 42 papers is about $55 and about 6 h wall-clock at two workers, before quota stalls. The human-minutes figure is the one that matters and it remains unmeasured (§3).
11. **The worklist ranks cells by what the extraction declares about itself.** A cell that declares itself a direct statement with an exact quote can be confidently wrong, and only the sampled P3 tier stands between that and an unexamined value; the sampled share is reported. By construction, 58 % of cells on the pilot received no budgeted human time because no registered result depends on them — that is a budgeting decision tied to the result list of the verification matrix, and if a result is added later the map in `output/stage_c_triage.py` must be re-run and the worklist regenerated.
