## Reasoning
The paper studies whether environmental-management standards (ISO 14001:2015,
EMAS III) contribute to energy management. Two components: (1) a qualitative
content analysis comparing the standards, and (2) an exploratory study of eight
Spanish hotels (four ISO 14001, four EMAS III).

Judged **ineligible** under the v2.3 threshold: it does not present, propose,
evaluate, or characterise an EnPI/EnB *measurement model*. EnPIs appear only as
(a) a required/proposed feature of the standards, (b) a binary practice recorded
for the hotels ("defined a set of energy performance indicators", p.16 / Table 5),
and (c) a catalogue of Energy Efficiency Indicators drawn from EMAS Sectoral
Reference Documents (Table 3, pp.13-14). No model construction/estimation
approach, variable-selection, or functional form is presented for any specific
model.

## Why this is borderline (flag for blind-first Tier-1 adjudication)
The competing (eligible) reading: Table 3 catalogues sector EEIs with their
Sectoral Units/denominators (e.g. "≤2.0 kWh per guest-night"; "≤40 kWh/m2yr"),
which are simple intensity-ratio indicators where the measured quantity and the
normalisation variable are identifiable. One could argue the content analysis
"characterises the indicators it covers." I judged against eligibility because
these are benchmark thresholds reproduced from the standards' reference
documents as *content of the standards*, not models the paper engages with as
measurement models; the paper's object is the standards' contribution to energy
management, and its conclusion critiques the *absence* of mandatory performance
indicators rather than any model. The borderline flag was NOT set because that
mechanism is scoped to `paper_type="review/synthesis"`, and this paper is a
standards content-analysis plus primary case study, not a review of >=3 prior
EnPI/EnB applications.
